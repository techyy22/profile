Autobiography

Childhood and Early Life

I was born in 2005 and grew up across the West Side of Chicago. I come from a large family, with four sisters and six brothers. Growing up with so many siblings taught me the importance of family, resilience, and looking out for the people around me.

In 2011, just two days before my birthday, my mother passed away. Her death was a major turning point in my childhood and deeply affected my entire family. My father did his best to care for all of his children while dealing with the loss himself. During that difficult period, my oldest sister stepped up and helped my father by taking custody of us and helping keep our family together.

In 2015, around the time of my mother's birthday, I experienced another devastating loss when my oldest brother was killed by gun violence. Losing my oldest brother at such a young age had a lasting impact on me and my family. With both my mother's death and my brother's death being connected to significant dates around birthdays, birthdays have never been the easiest time of year for me. They bring back memories of the people I've lost and the experiences my family has gone through.

Despite these hardships, I continued to move forward. I attended Lane Tech College Prep in Chicago, where I graduated in 2023 with a 4.8 weighted GPA. Graduating from Lane Tech was an important accomplishment, especially considering everything my family had experienced throughout my childhood.

That same year, I experienced another major loss when my father passed away just a couple of days after his birthday. Losing both of my parents and my oldest brother throughout my childhood and adolescence profoundly shaped who I am.

My early life was marked by loss, but it was also marked by family, perseverance, and the people who stepped up when things were difficult. Those experiences taught me resilience and independence and continue to influence the person I am and the future I am working to build.

Finding my Footing in Tech

It wasn't until my final year of high school that I realized technology was something I genuinely enjoyed. I had always been curious about computers and technology, but it was during my last year at Lane Tech that I began to see it as something I could actually pursue as a career.

That realization led me to Illinois Wesleyan University, where I decided to pursue a bachelor's degree in Computer Science. What started as an interest in technology quickly became a journey that has challenged me in ways I never expected.

College has not been easy. There have been sleepless nights, stressful days, moments of doubt, and plenty of times when I questioned whether I was capable of making it through. I've had to learn how to push through difficult classes, setbacks, and uncertainty while continuing to work toward the future I want.

But looking back, every late night and every difficult day has been worth it. I've grown not only as a computer science student, but also as a person. I've learned how to solve problems, ask questions, work through frustration, and keep going when things don't come easily.

Now, as I enter my senior year at Illinois Wesleyan, I'm able to look back at how far I've come with a different perspective. I still have a lot to learn and a long way to go, but I'm proud of the journey that brought me here.

I expect to graduate in 2027 with my bachelor's degree in Computer Science. What started as something I discovered during my final year of high school has grown into a path that I am genuinely excited to continue pursuing.
